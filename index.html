1 Start with the moment

“We’ll consider it carefully.”

The meeting felt polite. Nobody objected. Then the process slows down.

Workshop question: what did you assume happened — interest, delay, hidden rejection, or internal alignment?
The words are polite.
The signal is the work.

Rationale: Hall: meaning often sits in context. Hofstede: uncertainty avoidance makes vague risk expensive.

2 The operating shift

Stop asking: “Did they understand?” Ask: “Can they carry this safely?”

Old reading

Clear explanation + polite agreement = ready to move.

Better reading

Who needs confidence, what risk needs containment, and which channel lets disagreement appear without embarrassment?

Rationale: Theory explains why the second question matters. The slide gives managers a usable mental switch.

Live poll room diagnosis

Where do US managers usually misread Japan?

Rationale: The model names stay hidden; the room sees practical failure modes.

Reframe 1 Silence
Silence is not empty.

Old reading

They have no questions. I should keep explaining.

Better reading

They are processing, checking status, risk, and what can safely be said.

Manager move

Pause. Then ask: “Which concerns should we resolve before this becomes easy to recommend internally?”

Rationale: Hall makes the silence legible. Managers get one sentence to use.

Reframe 2 Questions

“Any questions?” is often the wrong question.

Weak prompt
“Does everyone agree this makes sense?”

Better prompt
“If you had to explain this internally tomorrow, what part would be hardest to defend?”

Do not demand public dissent. Give the room a face-saving way to name the risk.

Rationale: Hofstede/Hall explain the risk; the recommendation is a better question.

Practice Signal decoder

Translate the room.

“This may be difficult.”

Rationale: The exercise trains perception before strategy.

Reframe 3 Decision speed

Consensus is not bureaucracy. It is risk distribution.

If many people must live with the risk, many people must be able to live with the recommendation.

Manager move: include a stakeholder map before the proposal, not after it stalls.

Rationale: High uncertainty avoidance and long-term orientation make alignment part of quality control.

Practice Stakeholder map

Who needs confidence before the next step can travel?

Click the people who may need to carry or protect the recommendation after the meeting.

Selected: 0

Rationale: Models explain why title alone is not enough. The skill is stakeholder literacy.

Reframe 4 Feedback

Politeness is data.

“Interesting.”
Not yet convincing. Ask which condition would make it useful.
“We need to discuss internally.”
The recommendation needs a safer internal route or more senior alignment.
“It may be difficult.”
A blocker is already visible. Slow down and locate it.
“We understand.”
Understanding is not commitment. Confirm who can carry the next step.

Rationale: The cultural lens is a translation aid, not the content of the training.

Reframe 5 Risk

A pilot is only fast if it feels controlled.

Scope

What exactly is included?

Fallback

What happens if the result disappoints?

Quality gate

What must be true before expansion?

Reputation

Who can defend this internally?

Recommendation: frame the next step as a controlled learning path, not as a leap of faith.

Rationale: Hofstede gives the why; the manager gets a concrete checklist.

Practice Follow-up rewrite

Don’t chase. Make the next conversation easier.

Too direct / too pressuring
“Following up on our proposal. Can you confirm by Friday whether we can proceed?”

Rationale: Theory explains why pressure fails; the exercise trains better follow-up language.

6 Subtle red flags

Five signs a relationship is in trouble — that may look like success.

Excessive praise

Lots of politeness, no concrete next owner.

Sudden formality

Email gets more official and less human.

More CCs

Risk is being distributed defensively.

No small concerns

Either perfect — or nobody feels safe enough to name issues.

Meeting-only progress

Warm room, no internal carry path.

Fast verbal yes

Could be politeness unless operational owners confirm.

Rationale: Cultural models explain why warning signals are indirect.

7 Operating guideline

Make every recommendation easy to carry.

The best recommendation is not the one that sounds sharpest in the room. It is the one your counterpart can safely explain after you leave.

Rationale: This condenses Hall, Hofstede, Trompenaars, and WVS into one manager rule.

Commitment 30-day behavior

Three things to try next week.

Pause

Leave silence after important questions. Do not rescue the room too quickly.

Map

Name who must carry the recommendation internally.

Rewrite

Change one follow-up from “Can you confirm?” to “What concern should we help clarify?”

Final pair exercise: write one sentence you will actually use in a Japanese meeting.

Rationale: The deck ends with behavior change, not model recall.
